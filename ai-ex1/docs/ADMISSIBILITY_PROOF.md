For either kind of port cut in §5, the boundary consists of its selected port–ferry edges. A truck exits by BOARD and enters by DISEMBARK. For a piece, contract the bridge-free components to a tree. For sail counts, an incidence means one truck aboard during one particular sail. A last outward exit requires a departure with that truck aboard. Three details affect how to use that verdict.

1. **Dominated ports, lines 125–135.** A ferry can serve both an island and a leaf port (z) behind a dead bridge. Shared service alone does not prove (z) survives pruning. Use the explicit hypothesis at lines 125–127 that (z) is a permissible, nondead substitute for _every_ participating ferry. When substitutes are composed, every ferry involved in the first replacement serves the intermediate port, so it is also covered by the next replacement's check.
2. **DRIVE history, lines 33, 54 and 242–253.** “Focus flag” must include the actual previous road vertex. For a witness, use the road triangle (A,B,C), one empty truck starting at (C), and a package at (A) with goal (C). Reach (B) by (C o B) or by (C o A o B) without loading. The physical worlds coincide. In the former history a completion costs 4 by driving to (A), loading, driving to (C), and unloading. In the latter, the U-turn rule forces a DRIVE to (C) first, so the cheapest completion costs 5. An equality key that drops the predecessor would invalidate Lemma 5. The example establishes what the key must retain.
3. **Signed LOAD semantics, §11.** The assignment says the total weight must never exceed capacity (pp. 2–3). The document allows a negative package to be unloaded even when the remaining load then exceeds capacity. On the road (A-B-C), one capacity-1 truck starts at (A) with two packages lying there, of weights (-1) and 2 destined for (B) and (C). Loading both in that order and unloading at (B) and (C) costs 6 under the LOAD-only simulator interpretation. At (B) the truck is left holding weight 2. Under the continuous constraint, the weight-2 package must be unloaded before the negative one can be left at (B). Delivering at (C) and returning to (B) attains the minimum of 7: at least four handling actions are required, and a six-cost completion would have to make exactly the two forward drives with both packages handled just once in each direction of custody. Finally, the assignment uses two formulations of the pruning requirement. Page 6 permits reducing successors while preserving an optimal solution. Page 7 literally says to retain any action that may be part of an optimal solution. The pruning theorem proves existence of **one** surviving optimum. With two identical trucks at the same starting location and one package there requiring one road trip, loading either truck begins a three-action optimum. The lowest-ID rule keeps only one of these actions. This distinction affects pruning, independently of the admissibility of (h).
